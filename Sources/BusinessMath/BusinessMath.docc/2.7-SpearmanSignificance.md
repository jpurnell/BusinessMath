# Spearman Significance with Ties

Exact critical values and p-values for Spearman's rho between two rankings, with tied rankings handled exactly.

## Overview

Two judges rank the same `n` wines. Spearman's rho measures how well their rankings agree,
and the question is whether that agreement is more than chance. BusinessMath answers from
the **exact** null distribution wherever it can be counted, ties included, and says plainly
when it falls back to an approximation:

- ``spearmanTest(_:vs:alpha:method:)`` returns rho, its two-sided p-value, the critical
  values and the decision, ``SpearmanTestResult``.
- ``spearmanCriticalValues(items:alpha:method:)`` gives the untied table;
  ``spearmanCriticalValues(_:vs:alpha:method:)`` gives the critical values for two judges'
  own tie patterns. Both return ``SpearmanCriticalValues``.
- ``spearmanDistribution(items:)`` and ``spearmanDistribution(_:vs:)`` return the exact
  distribution itself, ``SpearmanDistribution``.

The inputs are the judges' scores or ranks, in the same order of wines. They are ranked
here with tied wines given the average of the places they span, exactly as
``spearmansRho(_:vs:)`` ranks them.

```swift
let first: [Double]  = [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8]
let second: [Double] = [2, 1, 4, 3, 6, 5, 8, 7]
let result = try spearmanTest(first, vs: second, alpha: 0.05)
result.rho              // 0.905
result.pValue           // exact, from all 8! = 40,320 arrangements
result.isSignificant    // true
result.criticalValues.upper   // 0.738: the untied 5% value for 8 wines

// A judge who could not separate wines 2 and 3 gets the critical values of that tie pattern.
let tied = try spearmanCriticalValues([1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8],
                                      vs: [1, 2.5, 2.5, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8], alpha: 0.05)
```

## The null hypothesis

One judge's ranking is held fixed, and the other's is a **uniformly random permutation of
that judge's own ranks**. Ties carry their averaged ranks, and **both judges' tie structures
are conditioned on as observed**: the distribution is the one for these two patterns of
ties, not for untied rankings.

The statistic is the Pearson correlation of the averaged ranks, which is what
``spearmansRho(_:vs:)`` computes. Under permutation both rankings' sums and sums of squares
are fixed, so rho orders the outcomes exactly as `Σ xᵢyᵢ` does, and in reverse as
`S = Σ dᵢ²` does. Without ties rho is the textbook `1 − 6S/(n³ − n)`.

## The exact method

The distribution is counted, not summed. One judge's doubled ranks are positions; the
other's are a multiset of values. A dynamic programme fills the positions one at a time, and
its state is how many of each value have been used. Every distinct arrangement is exactly
one path, every distinct arrangement is equally likely, and the final state holds the exact
count of arrangements at each value of `Σ xᵢyᵢ`. Van de Wiel and Di Bucchianico (2001,
J. Statist. Plann. Inference 92:133–145) compute the tied distribution by recursions of
this kind. Counts are held in multi-limb integers, so `n!` cannot overflow them.

Without ties the states are the `2ⁿ` subsets of values. Ties shrink that to `∏ (t + 1)`
over the tied groups of whichever judge has fewer states, so a heavily tied tasting is exact
well past the untied limit.

### The exactness guarantee

- The p-value is `min(1, 2·min(P(ρ ≤ r), P(ρ ≥ r)))`, the convention of R's `cor.test` and
  SciPy's `permutation_test`. Both tails are exact integer counts, and the smaller is doubled
  before the single rounding.
- Critical values compare each tail count with α/2 in exact integer arithmetic, α taken as
  the exact binary value of `alpha`, as the rank-sum critical values do
  (<doc:2.6-RankSumSignificance>). The bounds are the true critical values of the exact
  distribution.
- ``SpearmanTestResult/isSignificant`` is decided on the integer statistic, and agrees
  exactly with `pValue ≤ alpha` decided in integers.
- Reported rho values carry two roundings, a square root and a division, and perfect
  agreement is exactly ±1.

### Size

The exact method refuses, with `BusinessMathError.resourceExhausted`, a panel whose largest
layer would exceed 2²¹ states or whose two adjacent layers would exceed 2²⁵ limbs
(256 MiB). It checks before allocating any count table. Untied, that admits **17 wines**
and refuses 18, in a fraction of a second.

These figures are from a release build on Apple silicon, called from another module as an
app would call it, on a machine shared with other work (treat them as ±30%):

For a tasting of 4–12 wines the exact method costs nothing that matters. Beyond the budget,
use an approximation (next section) and say so.

## The convention

At two-sided level α:

- ``SpearmanCriticalValues/upper`` is the **smallest attainable** rho `U` with
  `P(ρ ≥ U) ≤ α/2`;
- ``SpearmanCriticalValues/lower`` is the **largest attainable** rho `L` with
  `P(ρ ≤ L) ≤ α/2`;
- a rho at or beyond either is significant. The bounds are themselves significant, which is
  how Zar's table reads ("reject if `r_s` ≥ the tabled value").

``SpearmanCriticalValues/nonSignificantRange`` gives the attainable rho values strictly
between them. ``SpearmanCriticalValues/sumOfSquaredDifferencesAtUpper`` and
``SpearmanCriticalValues/sumOfSquaredDifferencesAtLower`` give the bounds as `S`, for tables
printed that way. Without ties `L = −U`. With ties the distribution need not be symmetric:
the reference tests' eight-wine panel scored 1–5 by both judges has `L = −0.741` and
`U = 0.728`.

### When there are none

- **Too few wines.** With four untied wines the most extreme rho has probability 1/24, more
  than 0.025, so nothing is significant at 5% two-sided.
  ``SpearmanCriticalValues/canReachSignificance`` is `false`. At 10%, rho = ±1 is.
- **A judge who tied every wine.** Rho is `0/0`: there is no ranking to correlate.
  ``SpearmanTestResult/rho`` and ``SpearmanTestResult/pValue`` are `nil`,
  ``SpearmanTestResult/isComputable`` is `false`, and nothing is significant. Report this as
  "not computable", never as zero agreement.

## The approximations

Each is labelled and never chosen silently.

- ``SpearmanSignificanceMethod/edgeworth``: the AS 89 Edgeworth series (Best and Roberts,
  1975) as R implements it in `prho.c`, for **untied** rankings only. Ties are refused.
  Its critical values are the extreme even `S` whose Edgeworth tail is within α/2, found by
  bisection. That `S` need not be attainable.
- ``SpearmanSignificanceMethod/tDistribution``: `t = r·√((n − 2)/(1 − r²))` on `n − 2`
  degrees of freedom, computed as `I_{(1−r)(1+r)}((n − 2)/2, ½)` so nothing cancels near
  `|r| = 1`. It accepts ties.

### What R and SciPy do

R 4.6.2's `cor.test(method = "spearman", exact = TRUE)` is **exact only for n ≤ 9 without
ties**: `prho.c` enumerates all `n!` permutations up to `n_small = 9`. For 10 ≤ n ≤ 1290 it
uses the Edgeworth series, which is an approximation despite the argument's name, and above
1290 the t approximation. With ties it warns "cannot compute exact p-value with ties" and
uses t. SciPy 1.18.1's `spearmanr` uses t; its `permutation_test` is exact when it
enumerates.

### How far off they are

Measured against the exact distribution over every attainable rho, untied. The figures are
the largest absolute error in the two-sided p-value:

| n | Edgeworth (R from n = 10) | t (R `exact = FALSE`, SciPy) |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 0.080 | 0.150 |
| 6 | 0.019 | 0.048 |
| 8 | 0.0048 | 0.024 |
| 10 | 0.0011 | 0.013 |
| 12 | 0.00039 | 0.0083 |
| 14 | 0.00015 | 0.0060 |
| 16 | 0.000069 | 0.0042 |

Tied panels too large to count were checked against 10⁶ random permutations: 24 wines with
two tied pairs per judge, and 30 wines scored 1–20 against an untied ranking. The t p-value
was within 0.0033 of the permutation p-value everywhere. In the far tail it runs low, so it
is slightly anti-conservative there: at rho = 0.6 with 30 wines it gives 0.00046 against
0.00060 ± 0.00003.

## Validation

- **Brute force** over every one of the `n!` permutations, in the package's tests for
  n ≤ 8 tied and untied, and in Python integers for every fixture case up to n = 10.
- **An independent exact recursion** in Python integers, agreeing with brute force wherever
  both run, for 30 cases up to n = 20 tied, and exact critical values through n = 18.
- **Closed forms:** mean 0 and variance exactly `1/(n − 1)`, tied or not.
- **R 4.6.2:** exact p-values equal R's to 1e-12 for n ≤ 9 untied. The Edgeworth method
  reproduces R's `exact = TRUE` p-values for n ≥ 10 to a relative 1e-12, and the t method
  reproduces `exact = FALSE`.
- **SciPy 1.18.1:** `spearmanr` p-values, and the exact `permutation_test` for every
  observation with n ≤ 8, tied and untied.
- **Zar (1984) Table B.19** (after Zar 1972, JASA 67:578–580), transcribed from a scan of
  the book table, read as "reject if rho ≥ the tabled value", against the exact upper bound
  rounded to three decimals, at all nine levels from α = 0.50 to 0.001:
  - n = 4–16: **117 of 117** cells equal;
  - n = 17–18: 11 of 18 equal. The seven others differ by 0.002–0.005: at 17 wines and
    α = 0.05 Zar prints 0.485 where the exact value is 0.488 (and 0.615 against 0.618 at
    0.01, 0.662 against 0.659 at 0.005, 0.713/0.711 at 0.002, 0.748/0.743 at 0.001); at 18,
    0.695/0.692 at 0.002 and 0.728/0.725 at 0.001. Zar's values beyond 16 appear to come
    from an approximation; the exact values are the ones here. The package's tests recompute
    every cell through n = 17 with the exact method and pin these counts. Beyond 18 the exact
    references were not computed, because the Python reference needs more than 2 GB there.
- **Lee's table** (University of York, "Upper critical values of Spearman's rank correlation
  coefficient", computed by Lee rather than published): all 90 cells for n = 4–18 equal the
  exact values.
